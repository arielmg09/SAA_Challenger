The integration of Wealth and Asset Management into the Bank’s Model Risk Management framework this year means that, for the first time, the Strategic Asset Allocation model has been reviewed against the standards the Bank applies to all its models under PRA SS1/23. Reflecting this, most of our observations relate to how the model is documented, controlled and governed rather than to the investment thinking behind it. The SAA process reflects considerable expertise and a well-established investment rationale. What it lacks today is the formal evidence and control structure that allows that expertise to be demonstrated, reviewed independently and relied upon consistently from one year to the next. Closing that gap is the main purpose of this review.

To keep this manageable, we have grouped our findings into two priority areas for the next twelve months. The first, Governance, Documentation and Monitoring, focuses on recording how the model works and why, who owns and approves each step, and how its ongoing performance will be tracked. The second, Implementation and Data Controls, focuses on making the annual run more robust and repeatable: reducing reliance on manual transfers between workbooks, introducing independent checks on each run, and strengthening data quality controls. Our remaining observations on the Building Block and Historic Volatility methodologies will be addressed in a second phase, since they build directly on the documentation produced in the first.

MRM’s role is to support this work, not simply to assess it. We will agree a realistic remediation plan with clear owners and dates, share templates and examples from other model areas, and hold regular checkpoints so that issues are resolved along the way rather than at the next review. The aim is that by the model’s review anniversary, the SAA process is easier to run, less dependent on individual knowledge, and straightforward to defend to senior management, Internal Audit and the regulator. That is an outcome that benefits WAM as much as it does the Bank.
